What Glarion is

Glarion scans websites for security misconfigurations and turns the result into a report. It is aimed at agencies managing sites for clients, not at scanning domains you have no relationship with.

Who can use it

You must be at least 18 to open an account — we ask your date of birth at signup and check it. An account is for one person or organization; do not share login credentials across unrelated parties.

The one rule everything else depends on

You may only run a scan against a domain you own, or are authorized by its owner to test. Before any scan runs, you prove that by publishing a DNS record or file that only the domain's owner could place there. That proof expires after 30 days and is re-checked before every scheduled scan, because a domain can change hands.

We record the IP address, browser identifier, and timestamp behind every authorization, permanently, specifically so that "I didn't authorize that" can be checked against something. Falsely claiming ownership of a domain to run a scan is a breach of these terms and, depending on the outcome, may also be a criminal matter under computer-misuse law in your jurisdiction — that risk is yours, not ours; the verification step exists to keep it that way.

What the scans do and do not do

Scans are detection-only: they look for known misconfigurations and fingerprints. Nothing we run attempts to exploit a finding, brute force a credential, or degrade the target's availability. A scan can still generate real traffic against the target, and we rate-limit it for that reason — but you are still responsible for making sure the target can bear a security scan before you authorize one.

A clean report is not a guarantee of security. It means the checks we ran found nothing — not that no vulnerability exists. We re-rank findings by real-world severity rather than by scanner label, but that judgment can be wrong, and you are responsible for verifying anything you act on or pass to a client.
