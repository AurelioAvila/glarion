White-label website security audits for agency clients

Glarion runs automated external checks on client websites you have verified and turns the findings into a report under your agency’s name: what needs attention, why it matters and which task to assign next, with the evidence kept for their technical team. It is an automated audit, not a manual penetration test.

What goes into each report

  • Your agency’s name and, if you add one, your logo.
  • A short summary of what needs action first.
  • Prioritized findings, each with the affected site, the observed evidence, the practical impact and a recommended fix.
  • The scan date, the scope and the limits of the check.
  • A download as HTML, or a PDF saved from your browser.

How the audit runs

  1. Free public check. No account or card: the TLS certificate, security headers, SPF and DMARC records, cookie flags and public policy files, read exactly as every visitor sees them. What the free check covers.
  2. Verify domain control. Add a DNS TXT record or a hosted file. Proof lasts 30 days and is checked again before every scan, so full scans only ever run on domains you are authorized to manage.
  3. Full scans on a schedule. Detection-only vulnerability checks, run weekly or monthly, with an email when a result changes. Up to six full scans per site in any 24 hours (two a day during the trial).
  4. Send the report. Review the findings, then share the branded report with your client.

A structure that works

Start with a short executive summary: what changed, which issues need action and whether anything threatens availability or customer data. Follow it with prioritized findings. Each finding should contain the affected site, observed evidence, practical impact, recommended remediation and a clear confidence level.

Severity is only one part of priority

Technical severity describes potential harm. Operational priority also considers exposure, exploitability, confidence, business importance and the effort required to fix the issue. Reporting those factors prevents a long list of red badges from becoming a substitute for judgment.

A client should be able to tell the difference between “fix now,” “schedule next,” “verify manually” and “accept with a reason.”

White-label without hiding provenance

An agency report can carry the agency's identity while remaining honest about how evidence was collected. Include the scan date, scope, authorization state and limits of the assessment. A monitoring report is not a penetration-test certificate, and it should never imply otherwise.

Follow up on the work

Use scan history to compare observations after corrective work. Track the owner and remediation status in your team’s workflow; Glarion does not apply fixes. Inventory describes what was observed and should not be presented as proof that a check passed.
