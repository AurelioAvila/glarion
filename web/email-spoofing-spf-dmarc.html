SPF: what the policy means

SPF lists permitted sending sources for an envelope domain. An unmatched sender can produce a fail, soft fail or neutral result:

-all
Unlisted senders fail SPF. The receiver decides how to handle that result.
~all
Unlisted senders produce a soft fail; this does not require delivery or rejection.
?all
Neutral: no assertion about whether the sender is authorised.

Glarion summarises the published policy. It does not fully evaluate a message’s SPF result or validate every included sending service.

DMARC: the requested response

A message passes DMARC when SPF or DKIM passes with alignment to the visible From domain. For messages that fail DMARC, the policy requests:

p=reject
Rejection.
p=quarantine
Suspicious handling, such as a spam folder.
p=none
No DMARC-specific delivery action. Reports may be requested separately.

Receivers can apply their own policies. These records do not guarantee an inbox outcome or stop every kind of phishing, including lookalike domains.

Review before changing a policy

List the legitimate services that send for your business, including invoicing, newsletters and support. Review authentication and available DMARC reports before tightening a policy, so legitimate mail is not blocked.

The public check reads DNS records; it does not send test messages or change your email settings.
