From domain proof to a useful client update.

Active scanning begins only after authorization. Monitoring then turns repeat observations into a change-focused workflow.

  1. 1. Prove control

    Add a DNS TXT record or a file under /.well-known/. Proof expires after 30 days and is checked again when a scan runs.

  2. 2. Run controlled checks

    Choose weekly or monthly monitoring. Glarion uses a detection-only allowlist, caps scan intensity and refuses non-public network targets.

  3. 3. Review what changed

    Results are triaged into actions, decisions and inventory. Notifications are sent when a result differs from the previous one.

The scanner does not get unlimited freedom.

These controls are product behavior, enforced in code and covered by tests—not a claim that an automated scan is a penetration test.

Current authorization
Full scans require a plan and current DNS or hosted-file proof at queue time and execution time.
Public destinations only
IP literals, local names, private addresses, loopback ranges and cloud-metadata destinations are rejected.
Detection-only policy
Tools and templates are allowlisted. Fuzzing, brute force and denial-of-service categories are excluded.
Bounded frequency
Full scans are capped per target, and scheduled scans stop when domain verification expires.
Authorization trail
Each queued scan stores who authorized it and when, in the same transaction as the job.
Self-contained reports
Generated report values are escaped and the HTML document makes no external requests when opened.

What agencies usually ask.

What does the public check do?

It resolves the domain, reads its TLS certificate, requests the homepage and looks for public robots.txt and security.txt files. It uses GET requests, does not follow redirects and does not guess undisclosed paths.

Is Glarion a penetration test?

No. It is recurring security monitoring with controlled automated checks and triage. It does not replace a scoped manual penetration test.

Why verify a domain?

A full scan actively probes for known vulnerability fingerprints. Glarion requires proof that you control the target and checks it again when a queued or scheduled scan runs.

Can reports carry my agency’s brand?

Yes. Paid reports use your agency name and optional logo. They are self-contained HTML documents designed to print or save as PDF.

When are notifications sent?

Sites can run weekly or monthly. Glarion notifies you when a result differs from the previous one, so unchanged inventory does not create a repeated alert.

Does Glarion update plugins or take backups?

No. Keep your maintenance platform for updates, backups and uptime. Glarion focuses on outside-in security monitoring, triage and client-ready reporting.
